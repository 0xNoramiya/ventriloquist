## 1. Messages understood and communication difficulty (2025 review)

**Status: PARTLY.** Both figures appear in the review, worded as below. We could not confirm either one in the primary studies the review cites (details below). **Attribute both figures to the review, not to the primary studies.**

**README sentence**

> A 2025 review of communication tools in intensive care reports that only about 5% of the messages mechanically ventilated patients try to convey are correctly understood by healthcare providers, and that about half of ventilated patients report communication difficulties [1].

**Citation**

[1] Çelebi C, Öztepe Yeşilyurt K. Ensuring effective communication with patients receiving mechanical ventilation support in intensive care units: current communication materials. *Cyprus Journal of Medical Sciences*. 2025;10(4):228–235. doi:[10.4274/cjms.2025.2025-28](https://doi.org/10.4274/cjms.2025.2025-28). Article page: https://cyprusjmedsci.com/articles/doi/cjms.2025.2025-28. Narrative review, published 15 Aug 2025, open access (CC BY-NC 4.0).

**Quotes**

- p. 228, Introduction: "Only about 5% of the messages patients attempt to convey are correctly perceived and understood by healthcare providers. This indicates that communication difficulties reported by 50% of mechanically ventilated patients are compounded by comprehension and perception challenges.<sup>5,7</sup>"
- p. 234, Main Points: "Only about 5% of patients' messages are correctly understood, exacerbating communication challenges."

**Primary studies cited for these figures (references 5 and 7)**

The citation marks 5 and 7 come after the second sentence. The review does not say which reference supports which figure.

What the primary abstracts do report (these numbers are **not** approved for the README):

- Szymkowicz et al.: 44 ventilated patients in a crossover trial. The message success rate was 80% with a low-tech communication board and 100% with an eye-tracking device.
- Otuzoğlu & Karahan: 90 intubated patients after open-heart surgery. 77.8% of the intervention group rated picture-based material as helpful and 22.2% as partially helpful.

**Caveats for the README wording**

- Keep the attribution as "a 2025 review reports". The review does not describe how or where the 5% was measured, and we could not trace it to a primary measurement.
- Do not present the 5% as the success rate when communication aids are in use. One of the cited studies reports 80% success with a basic communication board, so the 5% most plausibly describes unaided communication. The review does not say this, so the README should not say it either.
- The review's wording is "perceived and understood". "Understood" is a fair shortening.

## 2. How ventilated patients communicated (observational ICU study)

**Status: VERIFIED** (against the PubMed Central full text of the same article).

**README sentence**

> In an observational study in an Iranian cardiac surgery ICU, most mechanically ventilated patients communicated through purposeful stares, hand gestures and head nods [2].

**Citation**

[2] Momennasab M, Shaker Ardakani M, Dehghan Rad F, Dokoohaki R, Dakhesh R, Jaberi A. Quality of nurses' communication with mechanically ventilated patients in a cardiac surgery intensive care unit. *Investigación y Educación en Enfermería*. 2019;37(2):e02. doi:[10.17533/udea.iee.v37n2e02](https://doi.org/10.17533/udea.iee.v37n2e02). PMID 31487439; PMCID [PMC7871491](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7871491/). Open access (CC BY). The PDF link originally provided (https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6970841.pdf) refused connections from our network. We checked the PMC full text instead, which is the same article (same DOI), so the README should link the DOI.

**Quotes**

- Abstract, Results: "the majority of patients used purposeful stares and hand gestures, and head nod for communication."
- Results section: "most of the communication methods used by patients (88.57%) were non-verbal expressive actions, including hand gestures and purposeful stares, followed by head nods (10.86%), with only one instance of writing by the nurse (0.57%)."

**Study context**

- 14-bed cardiac surgery ICU in Shiraz, Iran.
- 10 nurses and 35 patients.
- 175 observed nurse–patient communications.

**Other verified details (not on the approved list; get sign-off before using)**

- The percentages are shares of the 175 observed communications, not of patients.
- The paper also reports that "Assistive communication tools, such as word and picture boards, were not used."

## 3. SRAVI lip-reading app: Northern Ireland feasibility study

**Status: VERIFIED.**

**README sentence**

> In a Northern Ireland feasibility study of SRAVI, a smartphone app that reads lip movements, with tracheostomy patients, the version limited to a set phrase list was 21.8% accurate and the free-speech version 34.6%; the investigators concluded that a larger trial is not currently feasible [3].

**Citation**

[3] NHS Health Research Authority. Research summary: *Speech Recognition Application for the Voice Impaired (SRAVI) V1.0*. Full title: "A speech recognition application as a communication aid for critical care patients with tracheostomies". IRAS ID 307199. REC reference 22/NI/0109 (HSC REC B, favourable opinion 20 Jul 2022). ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT06027866](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06027866). Sponsor: Queen's University Belfast. Contact: Bronagh Blackwood. https://www.hra.nhs.uk/planning-and-improving-research/application-summaries/research-summaries/speech-recognition-application-for-the-voice-impaired-sravi-v10/ (accessed 2026-10-27).

**Quotes (Summary of Results)**

- "The first version of SRAVI, which relied on a set phrase list, had an accuracy of 21.8%, while the second version, allowing for free speech, achieved 34.6% accuracy."
- "The study concluded that a larger trial of SRAVI in acute and critical care settings is not currently feasible."
- From "Summary of Research", for the lip-reading description: the app "works by reading lip movements and translating them into words on the device screen."

**Caveats**

- This is an HRA lay summary of results, not a peer-reviewed paper.
- The summary does not define "accuracy" (for example top-1 or top-3). Do not compare these numbers directly with the 75% top-1 figure in fact 4.
- The summary reports 31 patients at four sites between January 2023 and April 2024, 84% of them from critical care. The trial registration describes "acute and critical care patients with tracheostomies". "Tracheostomy patients" is accurate. "ICU patients" alone would overstate it.

## 4. SRAVI: earlier single-centre evaluation

**Status: VERIFIED, with caveats that the README sentence should keep.**

**README sentence**

> An earlier single-centre evaluation of SRAVI with 14 tracheostomy patients reported 75% top-1 accuracy on phrases from the app's supported phrase list [4].

**Citation**

[4] Musalia M, Laha S, Cazalilla-Chica J, Allan J, Roach L, Twamley J, Nanda S, Verlander M, Williams A, Kempe I, Patel II, Campbell-West F, Blackwood B, McAuley DF. A user evaluation of speech/phrase recognition software in critically ill patients: a DECIDE-AI feasibility study. *Critical Care*. 2023;27:277. doi:[10.1186/s13054-023-04420-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-023-04420-x). Published 10 Jul 2023, open access (CC BY 4.0). Repository copy: https://clok.uclan.ac.uk/47850.

**Quotes**

- Abstract, p. 1: "Setting: Tertiary hospital critical care unit in the northwest of England. Participants: 14 patients with tracheostomies, 3 female and 11 male." and "The rank 1 recognition accuracy of the DNN method was 75%."
- Results, p. 4: "The rank 1 recognition accuracy of the DNN method is 75%, whilst the total recognition accuracy was 86%, accounting for all three ranked responses".
- Methods, p. 4: the single site was Lancashire Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, UK.

**Caveats**

- The 75% is for the deep-neural-network (DNN) method, at rank 1. The other method tested (DTW) reached 48% at rank 1.
- Accuracy was computed on 516 "phrase identifiable" recordings out of 616. Two of the 14 patients had no identifiable recordings.
- Patients mouthed phrases the app was built to recognise.
- 20 patients were recruited and 6 withdrew.
- The Discussion contradicts the Results in places: it reports 657 videos and "75% for DTW". The abstract and Results agree on 75% rank-1 accuracy for DNN, which is the figure used here.
- The evaluation was run jointly with Liopa, the app's developer, and Queen's University Belfast, and was funded by UKRI Innovate UK.
